## The Answer Up Front

**Daniel Rodriguez (+285 to +300) over Uroš Medić** — main event, welterweight, 5 rounds.

This is the only underdog on the card whose path to victory does not run directly into his opponent's single best statistical attribute. Every other live dog here is trying to do the one thing his opponent is elite at stopping. Rodriguez is trying to do the one thing his opponent has literally never done.

**Break-even at +285 is 26.0%. Medić has never in 16 professional fights seen a judge's scorecard, and has never fought a five-round bout.**

## Screening Method

I ranked every underdog on two axes and required both:

1. **Path clarity** — does the dog have a repeatable, statistically supported route to winning, and does that route survive contact with the favorite's strongest measured attribute?
2. **Price gap** — is the market's implied probability materially below a defensible true probability?

An underdog that clears only one axis is a lottery ticket, not a bet. Here is where each candidate landed.

## Eliminated: The Traps

### Jan Błachowicz +170/+225 — *pure name value, fade*

The most-bet underdog on this card and the worst one. Data:

| | Błachowicz | Stirling |
|---|---|---|
| Age | 43 | 28 |
| Record | 29-11-2 (12-8-2 UFC) | 10-0 (6-0 UFC) |
| Sig. strikes landed/min | 3.55 | **6.24** |
| Last victory | **May 2022** | Current 6-fight UFC streak |

Błachowicz is winless in his last four (per UFC pre-event facts; some outlets count it as five, depending on how the Ankalaev draw is treated) and has not had his hand raised in over four years. He is competing in his 23rd UFC light heavyweight bout — third-most in divisional history. That is a mileage number, not a durability number.

The only real argument for him is that Stirling took this on late notice off a Japan holiday. That is a narrative, not a statistic, and a 28-year-old undefeated fighter with a 2.7x output edge does not need a full camp to beat a 43-year-old who hasn't won since 2022. **No bet.**

### Tofiq Musayev +190/+215 — *path closed*

Genuinely tempting on résumé: RIZIN Lightweight Grand Prix champion, 23-6, 18 KO wins, and a unanimous-decision win over Ignacio Bahamondes in March 2026 earned via grappling control.

The problem is that the Bahamondes win *is* the tell. Musayev's UFC striking output is **1.84 significant strikes landed per minute** — he won that fight at 0.44 sig strikes/min by controlling on the mat. His entire route is takedowns and top position.

**Ľudovít Klein's takedown defense is 91%** — among the best in the division, to the point that Mateusz Gamrot publicly targeted the stat and failed to break it. Musayev averages 2.3 takedowns per 15 minutes against a man who stops nine of ten. At 1.84 sig strikes/min he cannot win this standing.

Musayev is also 36 and coming off a bloody, high-damage Fight of the Night. **The one thing he does runs into the one thing Klein does best. No bet.**

### Gilbert Urbina +275 — *wrong direction*

1-3 in the UFC, **finished in every one of his losses**, moving *up* to middleweight against a powerful pressure striker in Čepo's debut. A chinny fighter adding weight against a puncher is a fade, not a value play.

### Kyle Prepolec +475, Hailey Cowan +380 — *correctly priced*

Prepolec is 0-4 in the UFC. These are long prices because they should be.

### Poppeck +205, Vologdin +158, Buzukja +176, Todorović +140, Spasić +275 — *insufficient signal*

Poppeck is the most interesting of these — Sherdog picks him outright, and his opponent Jovan Leka is a UFC debutant. But Poppeck is *also* debuting, on short notice, moving up a weight class. Two debutants means no UFC-scale data on either side; that is variance, not edge. Buzukja is 1-4 in the UFC. Vologdin faces a short-notice but 16-2 opponent riding a six-fight streak who finishes nearly everyone, at home. None of these clear both axes.

## The Pick: Daniel Rodriguez +285/+300

### The structural case

Medić's record is the most unusual data point on this card:

> **Uroš Medić is 13-3. All 16 of those fights ended inside the distance. He has never once been to a decision as a professional.**

His 13 wins: 11 KO/TKO, 2 submissions, **0 decisions**. His 3 losses: all by stoppage, two by submission. Seven UFC wins, every single one by knockout. Three straight first-round KOs, most recently flattening Geoff Neal at 1:19 of round one in February 2026.

This means two things simultaneously:

1. Medić is exceptionally dangerous early — that part is fully priced at −360.
2. **There is no data on Medić after minute ~10 of a fight, at any point in his career.** Not "he's unproven in deep water" — genuinely zero observations.

And this is a **five-round main event. Medić has never fought one.**

### Why Rodriguez specifically exploits that

| | Medić | Rodriguez |
|---|---|---|
| Age | 33 | 39 |
| Record | 13-3 (7-3 UFC) | 20-5 (10-4 UFC) |
| Rank (WW) | #14 | #15 |
| Height / Reach | 6'1" / **71"** | 6'1" / **74"** |
| Stance | Southpaw | Southpaw |
| Sig. strikes landed/min | 5.11 | **7.20** |
| Sig. strikes absorbed/min | **3.55** | 5.31 |
| Net differential | +1.56 | **+1.89** |
| Decisions in career | **0** | Multiple, incl. wins over Holland, Morono, Li Jingliang |

Rodriguez lands **41% more volume per minute** than Medić and holds a **3-inch reach advantage**. In a mirror-stance southpaw matchup — where the open-stance angles that usually decide lefty-vs-righty fights are neutralized — reach and volume become the dominant variables, and Rodriguez owns both.

Rodriguez's route is unglamorous and repeatable: absorb the first two rounds behind a longer jab, drag Medić into rounds 3, 4 and 5, and win a fight that Medić's entire professional record says he has never had to have.

### Quality of opposition

Rodriguez's recent ledger is materially better than his price suggests:

| Date | Opponent | Result |
|------|----------|--------|
| Jul 19, 2025 | Kevin Holland | **W** — unanimous decision (UFC 318) |
| May 3, 2025 | Santiago Ponzinibbio | **W** — KO/TKO, R3 |
| Oct 12, 2024 | Alex Morono | **W** — split decision |
| Jun 22, 2024 | Kelvin Gastelum | L — unanimous decision (at MW) |
| May 13, 2023 | Ian Machado Garry | L — TKO, R1 (2:57) |
| Nov 5, 2022 | Neil Magny | L — submission (D'Arce), R3 |
| Sep 10, 2022 | Li Jingliang | **W** — split decision |

He also holds career wins over Kevin Lee and Mike Perry. Three straight wins over ranked or formerly-ranked welterweights, including a decision over Kevin Holland on a pay-per-view.

**Correction to a claim circulating in preview coverage:** several outlets state Rodriguez has "never been knocked out." That is false. Ian Machado Garry stopped him with strikes at 2:57 of round one in May 2023. His loss breakdown is 1 TKO, 1 submission, 3 decisions. He is durable — one stoppage loss by strikes in 25 professional fights — but he is not un-finishable, and any case built on that premise is built on bad data.

### Grappling and the second path

Medić's three losses are all stoppages and **two are submissions**. That is his measured floor: when Medić is in trouble, he does not grind out survival — he gets finished. Rodriguez holds a submission-era win history and, more importantly, this gives him a second route if the striking exchange goes badly. Medić has no equivalent fallback; his only demonstrated win condition is the finish.

## The Honest Case Against — and the Math After It

This is the part most previews are burying, so I'll put it plainly.

**Rodriguez has not fought since July 19, 2025 — a 12.5-month layoff. Roughly eight to nine of those months were spent in a Mexican prison** following an arrest for marijuana possession at the border. He was released in April 2026.

He trained while incarcerated — there is footage of makeshift bag work in his cell and mitts in the hallway — but he has said directly: *"Getting released from prison, I thought I was in shape until I started doing some of the stuff you normally do during training."* That is a fighter telling you his conditioning base was compromised.

Stack the genuine negatives:

- **39 years old**, oldest meaningful variable on the card
- **12.5-month layoff**, majority of it incarcerated, at an age where ring rust does not wash out in one camp
- **Absorbs 5.31 significant strikes per minute** — a leaky guard walking into a 100%-finish-rate knockout artist
- Has been stopped by strikes before (Garry)
- Fighting the home fighter in his home country's first UFC event
- Medić is a legitimate finisher, not a paper favorite

The compounding risk is specific and identifiable: **rounds 1 and 2.** Rodriguez's defensive numbers are exactly the vulnerability Medić's game is built to exploit, and Medić's last three wins all ended in round one.

**Now the math.** A statistical model does not price eight months of incarceration. So discount it manually:

| Scenario | Rodriguez true win % | Fair price | Edge at +285 |
|----------|---------------------|-----------|--------------|
| Model, undiscounted | 39% | +156 | Large |
| Moderate layoff discount | 33% | +203 | Solid |
| Heavy layoff discount | 30% | +233 | Positive |
| **Break-even** | **26.0%** | **+285** | — |

Even applying a **nine-point haircut** to the model — a severe penalty, treating the prison layoff as costing Rodriguez nearly a quarter of his modeled win probability — he still clears the break-even bar by four points.

**That is the whole thesis: the bar is 26%, and a ranked top-15 welterweight with three straight wins over quality opposition, a 41% volume edge, a 3-inch reach edge, and an opponent who has never seen round four does not lose this 74+% of the time.**

## Secondary Play: Marcin Tybura +250/+300 (lower conviction)

Worth stating because the favorite's profile is genuinely alarming:

**Aleksandar Rakić has not won a fight since March 2021.** He is 1-4 in his last five, on the longest losing streak of his career:

| Opponent | Result |
|----------|--------|
| Jan Błachowicz (2022) | L — blew out his knee, ~2-year layoff |
| Jiří Procházka (UFC 300, Apr 2024) | L — TKO |
| Magomed Ankalaev (Oct 2024) | L — decision |
| Azamat Murzakanov (UFC 321, Oct 2025) | L — **TKO, R1**, dropped by a jab |

He is now making his **heavyweight debut after 20 professional fights and 11 straight octagon appearances at 205**, having said his "body needed to recover" from the cut. His significant strike defense is **51%**. He is a −300 to −360 favorite on that résumé.

**Why it's only secondary:** Tybura's path is grappling, and it hits the same wall that eliminated Musayev. Tybura lands takedowns at **33.8% accuracy**; **Rakić's takedown defense is 86%**. Tybura is also 40, on a two-fight skid, and was knocked out in round one by Ante Delija in September 2025 before dropping a decision to Tyrell Fortune in March 2026 — so his own chin is a live question against a man with nine KO wins.

There is also a counter-argument the market may be reading correctly: moving *up* in weight removes a cut that was visibly destroying Rakić, and Tybura is an undersized heavyweight facing a man who was oversized at light heavyweight. The size angle may not favor Tybura at all.

Live dog on the favorite's decay. Not a clear path. **Small stake at +280 or better, or pass.**

## What I Could Not Verify

Direct fetches to ufcstats.com, Sherdog, Tapology, ESPN, BestFightOdds, MMAOddsBreaker, FightBookMMA, RotoWire, UFC.com and Wikipedia **all returned HTTP 403 at this environment's egress proxy** — an organization network-policy block, not a site outage. I did not route around it. Every figure in this document therefore comes from search-result extracts referencing those sources rather than from the primary statistical tables.

Specific gaps a serious bettor should close before staking:

- **Weigh-in results were not yet published** at the time of writing (weigh-ins are the morning of July 31; the event is August 1). **Confirm Rodriguez made 170 cleanly** — a 39-year-old off a 12.5-month layoff and eight months of prison food is a genuine weight-cut risk, and a bad cut would invalidate the entire deep-water thesis this pick rests on. This is the single highest-value unresolved variable.
- Round-by-round striking and control-time splits for Medić were unavailable; his "no data past round 2" profile is inferred from his 0-decision record, not from tracked per-round output.
- No verified injury report or camp-change reporting for Rodriguez beyond his own public comments on conditioning.
- Błachowicz's winless streak is reported as four by UFC pre-event facts and five by some outlets, depending on treatment of the Ankalaev draw. Both readings support the same conclusion.
- Elliott–Oliveira odds were not located.
- The VSiN model's 39% for Rodriguez is a third-party output I could not independently reproduce or inspect. Its cited 2026 record (88-50-6) is likewise unverified. It is one input, not the thesis — the structural case stands on Medić's 0-decision record independently.
