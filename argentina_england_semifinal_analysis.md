**The pace-profile story:** England is the high-output direct team — most sprints, most crosses, most runs in behind among surviving sides — yet has **zero fast-break goals**. Its scoring is slow-build wide service: 21.5 cross attempts per game, six of its goals involving headers, five involving set pieces. Argentina is the opposite — half England's sprint count, the most completed passes in the tournament, and the field's most lethal transition unit (4 fast-break goals). Argentina doesn't need to press; England doesn't score on the break.

Play that matchup forward: Argentina holds the ball (someone must cede possession, and Tuchel's England has been comfortable at 52% against weaker sides than this), England sits in a mid-block and goes direct to Kane and the wide channels, Argentina declines to chase the game and waits to counter. England's best chances arrive as crosses into **Romero and Lisandro Martínez**; Argentina's arrive on counters England's shape is specifically built to deny. That is a low-chance-volume script on both ends — exactly what the books' 2.1–2.2 goal projection is pricing.

**Both teams' goal profiles skew dead-ball** — England: 5 of 13 goals set-piece-involved; Argentina: Mac Allister's header off a Messi corner opened the QF, and a Messi corner produced the R32 extra-time winner (Romero's header, credited as an own goal). Set-piece-dominated tight games land on 0-0, 1-0, and 1-1 far more often than open ones.

## Why Not Just Bet the Under?

Because the books got there first. Under 2.5 at -170/-179 de-vigs to ~60%, which **is** roughly the fair number for this script — meaning the value is gone and you're paying full tax to agree with the house. The repo's standing principle applies: when a side is fully priced, the edge isn't the thesis, it's finding the *underpriced expression* of the thesis.

The draw **is** the underpriced expression. Draw-heavy scripts (0-0, 1-1) are a subset of under scripts, and the draw pays **+190** instead of charging -170 — with the day's sharpest line movement behind it rather than against it.

Honest math: at the books' ~2.2 total goals split evenly, independent Poisson gives ~29% draw; low-scoring correlation (Dixon-Coles) adds 3–5 points; the knockout-specific "settle for extra time" effect — both of these exact teams did it four days ago — adds more late-game convergence that no Poisson captures. A fair range is **33–36%** against a 34.5% breakeven at +190. That's a genuine but thin edge, which is why this is a 0.5–1 unit lean and why price discipline matters: **+190 is the floor. At +180, pass.** The sharp entry was +200; you're already paying some steam.
