## Bottom Line Up Front

**The underdog with the clearest structural path to victory is MARCIN TYBURA (+272 book average, shop for +300 to +317) against Aleksandar Rakić.**

Rakić is being priced as a live contender (≈77% implied) while carrying **a four-fight losing streak, two knockout losses in his last three appearances, a nine-month layoff, and a first-ever move up to heavyweight.** Tybura is a durable, twenty-fight veteran of the division Rakić is only now entering.

**Conviction: modest. This is a ~0.5–1 unit play, not a strong one.** Two honest counter-signals keep it from being more: Rakić's **90% career takedown defense** blunts Tybura's primary path, and **the market has moved toward Rakić, not away from him** — which is normally the signal you want on your side, not against you. I am recommending the best available underdog on a thin card, not a mispriced lock.

This card is heavy chalk: **nine of thirteen lined bouts have a favorite at -250 or shorter.** Most underdogs here are underdogs for correct reasons. If you can't get Tybura at **+280 or better, pass entirely.**

## Why the Rest of the Field Was Eliminated

Working through every underdog on the board, in order of price:

**Daniel Rodriguez (+325, main event)** — The tempting headline number, and the worst bet on the card. He is **39 years old**, returning from a **year-long layoff during which he served roughly eight months in a Mexican prison** (arrested crossing the border with marijuana; released in April). He then walks into a five-round main event against a fighter whose **last three wins are all first-round knockouts** (Urbina, Salikhov, Neal), in that fighter's home country, in front of a home crowd. Rodriguez is a genuinely excellent volume boxer with one of the higher career significant-strikes-landed rates in UFC history — but incarceration is not a training camp. Layoff, age, ring rust, and a five-round distance against a finisher is the wrong side of every variable at once. VSiN called the main-event moneyline "completely unplayable" in both directions. Agreed.

**Oban Elliott (+290)** — On a two-fight skid, was **knocked down and submitted by Jonathan Micallef and suffered a broken orbital** in the process, causing another extended layoff. Now faces an unbeaten DWCS graduate (9-0, eight finishes) who dominated a current UFC welterweight to earn his contract. Coming off a facial fracture against a finisher is a fade, not a play.

**Gilbert Urbina (+275/+300)** — 7-4 overall and has **lost two straight by first-round stoppage**, one of them to Uroš Medić. Facing a 14-3 Serbian with a **100% career finish rate** in his home arena. No.

**Jan Błachowicz (+245/+258)** — This is the sentimental pick, and it is the trap. The former champion is **43 years old and on a five-fight winless streak with no victory since May 2022** (two draws, two narrow decision losses, most recently a majority draw with Bogdan Guskov). The statistical gap is stark: **Stirling lands 6.24 significant strikes per minute at 54% accuracy; Błachowicz lands 3.55 at 50%.** Stirling is 28, 10-0, on a six-fight UFC win streak, and 15 years younger. The one point in Błachowicz's favor is that **Stirling took the fight on short notice**, reportedly straight off a holiday in Japan — but Błachowicz's documented late-career problem is his *own* gas tank, which means he is poorly built to punish an underprepared opponent in rounds four and five. Name value is doing the work in that +250 price. Fade.

**Tofiq Musayev (+168/+190)** — The most interesting near-miss. 23-6 with **18 wins by KO/TKO**, a former RIZIN lightweight Grand Prix champion, coming off a decision win over Ignacio Bahamondes. But Klein is legitimately good and in form: **5-1 in his last six**, coming off a Fight of the Night majority decision over Mateusz Rębecki, and he holds edges in both volume (3.76 vs 1.84 significant strikes per minute) and accuracy (53% vs 46%). Musayev's output number is a two-fight UFC sample and probably understates him, but there is no clear structural mispricing here — just a puncher's chance. Pass.

**Dennis Buzukja (+174/+210)** — **1-4 in the UFC**, on a two-fight skid including a **first-round knockout loss to Marcio Barbosa in April**. Grad is also skidding, but has the better power and finishing profile (nine KOs in fifteen wins). Two fighters fighting for their jobs; no edge worth taking.

**Duško Todorović (+126/+140)** — **The legitimate secondary play, and the closest thing to a "best value" argument against my primary pick.** Valentin is a -162 favorite despite being **1-3 in the UFC**, against a Todorović who is 4-6 but has a real wrestling edge (2+ takedowns in three of his last six). Home crowd in Belgrade. The problem is durability: **Todorović has been finished in four of his six losses**, and Valentin is a lunging finisher with submission instincts who just tapped Leblanc quickly. You are getting a modest price to bet on a fragile chin holding up. Playable at +140, not at +126.

**Gugnon (-117), Prepolec (+475), Cowan (+380), Spasić (+275), Poppeck (+205), Vologdin (+158)** — Gugnon isn't an underdog (he's the betting favorite despite nominal dog status, and RotoWire expects him to close favored). Prepolec against Rębecki and Cowan against Milošević are lottery tickets priced roughly correctly. The remainder are low-information prospect-vs-journeyman spots where I have no verified edge and won't manufacture one.

### Recent Form — Quality of Opposition

**Rakić has lost four consecutive fights**, the longest skid of his career:
- **Jan Błachowicz** — TKO loss, R3, 2022 (suffered a catastrophic knee injury; sidelined nearly two years)
- **Jiří Procházka** — TKO loss, R2, UFC 300 (April 13, 2024)
- **Magomed Ankalaev** — decision loss, UFC 308 (October 2024)
- **Azamat Murzakanov** — **TKO loss at 3:11 of round one**, UFC 321 (October 25, 2025)

The opposition quality is elite — two of those are current or former champions — which is the strongest argument for Rakić's price. But the *manner* matters: **he has been stopped in three of the four, twice in his last three fights, and most recently inside one round.**

**Tybura is 2-3 in his last five:** a decision win over then-undefeated Mick Parkin, a knockout loss to Delija, and a **unanimous-decision loss to Tyrell Fortune on March 28, 2026 — in Fortune's UFC debut.** That last result is the single ugliest data point in this thesis and I'm not going to soften it.

### The Case For Tybura

1. **Four straight losses is not priced in at -344.** A fighter who has not won since 2022, has been knocked out in round one of his most recent fight, and is changing weight class should not be a 77% favorite over a ranked opponent. This is a market pricing *reputation and opponent quality* rather than *current form*.

2. **The heavyweight move is a genuine unknown, and unknowns favor the dog.** Rakić has fought all **20 professional fights at 205 pounds**. His own stated reason for moving is recovery — *"my body needed to recover."* That is a fighter telling you the cut was destroying him, which is a legitimate reason to expect a fresher fighter — but it is equally a fighter who has never carried heavyweight weight, never absorbed a heavyweight punch, and never had to move a genuine heavyweight's mass off him.

3. **The 90% takedown defense number is less predictive than it looks.** It was compiled entirely against light heavyweights. Tybura is **249 pounds against a career 205er**, with matched 78-inch reach — meaning Rakić's usual length advantage evaporates and he faces roughly 30–40 extra pounds in every clinch and fence exchange. Takedown-defense percentages are notoriously weight-class-dependent.

4. **Durability and chin.** Rakić has been finished by strikes twice in three fights. Tybura has **ten career knockouts**. Tybura does not need to out-grapple him — he needs Rakić's chin to be exactly as compromised as recent evidence suggests.

5. **Cardio and championship-round experience at heavyweight.** Tybura has built a career on a strong gas tank *by heavyweight standards*, wearing down prospects into the later rounds. Rakić has never had to manage a heavyweight fight's energy demands.

6. **Layoff and instability.** Rakić last competed **October 25, 2025 — a nine-month layoff** — and that fight followed a **camp/team change** that he publicly framed as unlocking his "true potential." It ended in a first-round knockout. He is now on his second consecutive major change (team, then division) while trying to end a four-fight skid.

7. **Divisional experience.** Tybura has shared the cage with the heavyweight division's genuine elite over a long career. Rakić has shared it with none of them.

### The Case Against Tybura (Steelman)

I'd be selling you something if I left these out:

- **Rakić's 90% takedown defense is still real.** Even discounted for the weight change, Tybura's primary path — 1.30 takedowns per 15 minutes at 33% accuracy — is not a high-volume wrestling attack. If the grappling cancels out, this becomes a kickboxing match, and Rakić is the better and more powerful technical striker.
- **Tybura's chin is a documented, long-standing weakness.** He has been visibly hurt and stopped by single shots before (Abdurakhimov, Sakai). Rakić's one elite trait is power.
- **Tybura is 40 and lost to a UFC debutant four months ago.** The decline is not hypothetical.
- **Tybura's output is low.** If this goes to the cards on volume, he does not have the striking rate to bank rounds.
- **The market is against you.** See below — this is the most important counter-signal.

The counter-reading — and it is a *reading*, not a fact — is that the drift is consistent with **public money backing a recognizable name in a Balkan arena on a card being sold to a regional audience**, against a low-profile Polish gatekeeper. Serbian and regional betting interest on a hometown-adjacent card is a plausible source of one-directional flow. I cannot verify handle-versus-ticket splits to prove that, so I am flagging it as a hypothesis, not evidence.

**Practical consequence: the drift is why this is a half-to-one-unit play instead of a two-unit play. It also means the number is getting better for you — +317 is available where +222 was the open.** Take the best number you can find.

### Stylistic Path to Victory

The winning script for Tybura is concrete and doesn't require anything exotic:

1. **Close distance early and make it heavy.** Force clinch and fence exchanges in round one where the 40-pound mass differential is decisive and Rakić's kicking range is neutralized.
2. **Body work and volume against the fence** to tax a fighter who has never managed heavyweight energy expenditure.
3. **Land one clean shot on a chin that has failed twice recently.** Tybura's ten knockouts make this a live secondary path, not a fantasy.
4. **Drag it into round three.** Rakić's last fight lasted 3:11. He has gone the distance once in his last four. Deep water is where the layoff, the new weight, and the four-fight psychological burden all compound.

The losing script is equally clear: Rakić keeps it at range, defends the takedown at his career rate, banks rounds with the jab and leg kicks, and either wins a decision or catches a slow, aging opponent. **That is the more likely single outcome — I just don't think it's 77% likely.**

### My Estimate

I make Tybura roughly **33–37%** to win, against an implied **26.9% at +272** and **24.0% at +317**. That is a real but unspectacular edge, entirely dependent on the view that four straight losses plus a weight-class change plus a nine-month layoff is being underpriced.

### What Would Make Me Pass

- **Can't get +280 or better.** At +272 or shorter the edge is inside my error bars.
- **Tybura misses weight or looks visibly drained on the scale** — weigh-in results were not yet published at time of writing.
- **Any report that Rakić has genuinely rebuilt his frame at heavyweight** (i.e., he weighs in at 240+ and looks powerful rather than merely un-cut).
- **Confirmation of a Tybura injury or a late camp disruption.**

## Narrative Traps to Avoid on This Card

- **"Błachowicz is a former champion at +250, that's free money."** He has not won a fight in over four years, is 43, and has documented cardio decline against a 10-0 opponent who out-strikes him nearly 2:1 by volume. Name value, not value.
- **"Rodriguez is a proven veteran and D-Rod always shows up."** He was in prison. Eight months. Then a five-round main event against a first-round finisher in that man's home country.
- **"Rakić is 0-4, just blindly fade him."** This is the mirror-image error to the one I'm exploiting. Those four losses came against Błachowicz, Procházka, Ankalaev, and Murzakanov — a murderers' row. The fade is justified by *how* he lost and *what has changed*, not merely by the record.
- **"Todorović is fighting at home, ride the crowd."** Crowds don't fix chins.
