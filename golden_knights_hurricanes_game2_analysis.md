## Why the Public Loves the Under (and why it's a trap, not a lock)

The Under case is legitimate — which is exactly why 91% of the public is on it:

1. **Goalie regression.** Two elite goalies posted ≈ -1.7 GSAx each in Game 1. Mean reversion says both are better in Game 2.
2. **Carolina trends Under.** The total has gone Under in **13 of Carolina's last 16** games. Elite defense + elite PK + weak finishing = low-event hockey.
3. **Analyst consensus** is for a tighter, defensively-detailed Game 2 with "better goaltending for both teams."
4. **Carolina's broken power play** removes one scoring avenue entirely (0 shots on 4:00 of PP in Game 1).

**The flaw:** every one of these is already baked into a number the public has hammered to 91% of tickets — and the line still won't drop below 5.5. When a side is this crowded and the number won't move, you are getting a **worse-than-fair price on the popular side and a better-than-fair price on the other.** Over -132 implies the book's true expectation is **north of 5.5 goals** (≈ 5.7+); the book is pricing the Over up even as the public buys the Under down.

## Why the Over is the Sharper Number

The Over does **not** require bad goaltending. It requires **chance volume**, and this matchup manufactures it:

1. **Carolina's volume is elite and repeatable.** A 59% Corsi team generates a flood of shots and chances regardless of finishing luck. Positive finishing regression (30 goals on 37 xGF) points *up*, not down — Carolina is "due" to score more, not less.
2. **Vegas's power play is a 23.9% weapon and Carolina takes penalties.** Special-teams goals are the cleanest path over a total, and Vegas owns that lever.
3. **Vegas is getting healthier, not thinner.** **Mark Stone is back** and **Lauzon returns** (replacing Korczak on the blue line) after a 10-game absence. More firepower, more depth.
4. **Both defenses looked vulnerable in Game 1.** "Carolina's typically impenetrable defense looked very vulnerable" — and Vegas's blue line just put up six points from two defensemen.
5. **The money agrees.** 40% of handle on 9% of tickets, plus a line held at 5.5 with the Over juiced — sharp money is on the Over.

The honest synthesis: a "true" projection for this game sits right around **5.7–6.0 goals.** That's barely over the number — the *projection* edge is small. The *market* edge is what makes the Over the play: you're on the same side as the money against a public that's overpaying for a narrative.
